Medusa
======

Medusa is one of the most iconic figures in Greek mythology. She is a Gorgon —
a creature with living venomous snakes in place of hair — whose gaze was so
terrifying that anyone who looked directly into her eyes was turned to stone.

Origin
------
In the most widely known version of her myth, Medusa was originally a beautiful
mortal woman and a priestess of Athena. She was transformed into a monster by
Athena as punishment after Poseidon assaulted her in Athena's temple.

Appearance
----------
As a Gorgon, Medusa is typically depicted with:
  - A female human face surrounded by writhing serpents for hair
  - Brazen claws and wings (in some accounts)
  - A gaze that petrifies all living creatures who meet it

Death & Legacy
--------------
Medusa was slain by the hero Perseus, who used a mirrored shield gifted by
Athena to avoid her direct gaze. He beheaded her while she slept, and from her
severed neck sprang the winged horse Pegasus and the giant Chrysaor — children
she had conceived with Poseidon.

Perseus later used Medusa's severed head as a weapon, turning the sea-monster
Cetus to stone in order to rescue Andromeda, before gifting the head to Athena,
who placed it on her aegis (shield) as the Gorgoneion — a protective emblem.

Cultural Significance
---------------------
The image of the Gorgoneion became a powerful apotropaic symbol in ancient
Greece, displayed on temples, armour, and coins to ward off evil. In modern
culture Medusa remains a widely referenced figure in art, literature, film,
and psychology, often reinterpreted as a symbol of female rage or victimhood.

Key Facts
---------
  - Species  : Gorgon (one of three sisters; the only mortal one)
  - Sisters  : Stheno and Euryale (immortal)
  - Slayer   : Perseus
  - Offspring: Pegasus and Chrysaor (born at death)
  - Symbol   : Gorgoneion
